Rollforward and Point-in-Time Recovery

Apply logs to end-of-logs or a chosen recovery time, and make the terminal completion decision deliberately.

Level: Advanced · Effort: 3 hours
Prerequisites: F-01, F-05/F-06; isolated restored database and complete logs.
Version scope: Db2 LUW 12.1 is the primary reference. Confirm syntax and monitor columns against the installed fix pack; 11.5 compatibility is stated only where checked.
Outcomes · How it works · Examples and commands · Diagram · Known issues and fixes · Lab · Review

Learning outcomes

  • Read rollforward status and identify required logs.
  • Choose end-of-logs or point-in-time recovery.
  • Explain timezone and minimum-recovery-time constraints before stopping.

How it works and when to use it

RESTORE rehydrates an image; ROLLFORWARD applies transactions logged after the image. QUERY STATUS reports progress and the next log. END OF LOGS and point-in-time recovery are different decisions: once end-of-logs processing begins, switching to a point-in-time target requires another restore. Db2 interprets timestamps according to documented UTC/local-time rules. A tablespace restore can have a minimum recovery time based on catalog updates. `SQL4970N` may be returned when the point-in-time request lacks the later-transaction evidence required by RECOVER.

Decision rule: Before STOP/COMPLETE, confirm image time, log chain, recovery timestamp, timezone, minimum recovery time, and business data-loss boundary.

Worked examples and commands

Inspect current rollforward requirement

db2 rollforward db SAMPLE query status

Expected / interpretation: Record state, last committed transaction, and next log. If the named log is missing, locate it before deciding to stop.

Apply logs to end-of-logs and complete

db2 rollforward db SAMPLE to end of logs
db2 rollforward db SAMPLE stop

Expected / interpretation: Use only when end-of-logs is the approved target and required logs are accessible. The second command completes recovery after successful application.

Recover to a planned time

db2 rollforward db SAMPLE to 2026-10-26-22.30.00 and stop

Expected / interpretation: This sample uses the documented timestamp shape. Confirm UTC versus local time and the application transaction boundary before execution.

Recovery decision flow: Select target, apply logs, verify, then stop

Completion is gated on a proven image, log chain, timezone, and business recovery point.

Known issues, errors, fixes, and verification

Connection returns `SQL1117N` after restore.

Likely cause: The database is rollforward pending and required log application is incomplete.

Fix: Run QUERY STATUS, retrieve the next required log, and follow the approved end-of-logs or PIT sequence.

Verify: Status reports completion, connection succeeds, and application checks show the expected recovery point.

Point-in-time recovery returns `SQL4970N`.

Likely cause: The target timestamp cannot be established from available transaction/log evidence; the database may remain pending.

Fix: Compare target time with image completion, available logs, timezone, and transaction markers. Choose a supported target only after the recovery owner approves the data-loss boundary.

Verify: Rehearse the chosen timestamp in an isolated restore and confirm pre/post-target transactions.

Recovery stops at the wrong business time.

Likely cause: UTC/local interpretation or daylight-saving transition was misread.

Fix: Convert the requested time using the server timezone and daylight-saving rule; record the selected interpretation.

Verify: Use status and a known transaction marker before opening the database to applications.

Hands-on lab: Choose a recovery target from evidence

Objective: Recover a synthetic database to a known transaction boundary.

  1. Restore a disposable full image and capture its timestamp.
  2. Inspect rollforward status and enumerate available logs in order.
  3. Choose END OF LOGS or a UTC point from a supplied timeline.
  4. Apply logs, complete recovery, and query markers committed before and after the target.

Answer key: The target must be later than the online backup completion where applicable, within the available chain, and aligned with a business boundary. A post-target marker must be absent in a point-in-time result.

Cleanup: Restore the lab image again before repeating an incorrect target.

Review question and answer

Question: Why is PIT timestamp selection a data-loss decision?

Answer: It determines which committed transactions are included; a wrong timezone or target can omit work or constrain later recovery.
