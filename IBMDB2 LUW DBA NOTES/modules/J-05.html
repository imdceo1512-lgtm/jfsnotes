Scheduling, Alerting, Dashboards, and Operational Reporting

Schedule Db2 jobs with missed-run handling and build actionable alerts tied to service objectives.

Level: Advanced · Effort: 2 hours
Prerequisites: E-06 and J-02.
Version scope: Db2 LUW 12.1 is the primary reference. Confirm syntax and monitor columns against the installed fix pack; 11.5 compatibility is stated only where checked.
Outcomes · How it works · Examples and commands · Diagram · Known issues and fixes · Lab · Review

Learning outcomes

  • Inspect cron/systemd scheduling and execution context.
  • Design alerts with duration, baseline, ownership, and runbook.
  • Report job freshness and success rather than process existence alone.

How it works and when to use it

A scheduled job runs with a different identity, environment, PATH, timezone, and secret context than an interactive shell. Cron and systemd timers have different catch-up and logging behavior. A green process does not prove the Db2 operation succeeded. Alerts need a measured service threshold, persistence window, freshness check, owner, severity, and action link; alert noise and missing data require separate states.

Decision rule: Use systemd timers for managed services where available or cron for simple schedules; pin environment and credentials, log structured status, detect missed/stale runs, and exercise alert routing before production.

Worked examples and commands

List configured systemd timers

systemctl list-timers --all

Expected / interpretation: Linux systemd example; not available on all UNIX platforms. Confirm timer unit and next/last activation.

Inspect a scheduled service journal

journalctl -u db2-healthcheck.service --since "1 hour ago"

Expected / interpretation: Use the actual unit name; correlate exit status, Db2 identity, and output freshness.

Inspect current user cron entries

crontab -l

Expected / interpretation: Run as the scheduling identity and compare PATH, DB2INSTANCE, and timezone with the intended configuration.

Scheduled monitoring flow: Job execution and data freshness are separate health checks

Test the job, the metric, the stale-data alarm, and the notification route independently.

Known issues, errors, fixes, and verification

A job succeeds interactively but fails when scheduled.

Likely cause: Different profile, PATH, permissions, environment, credentials, working directory, or timezone.

Fix: Use an explicit wrapper with a controlled environment and service identity; log environment identifiers without secrets.

Verify: Run under the scheduler account and compare current server/user and effective config.

No alert fires when collection stops.

Likely cause: The dashboard treats missing data as zero or only checks metric value, not freshness.

Fix: Add a separate stale-data/heartbeat alert with owner and escalation path.

Verify: Disable a lab collector and confirm a missing-data alert arrives within the stated interval.

Too many transient alerts are ignored.

Likely cause: Threshold lacks a duration, baseline, dependency grouping, or user-impact severity.

Fix: Require sustained breach or correlated symptoms and route to a specific runbook owner.

Verify: Replay normal and incident data and measure noise and detection delay.

Hands-on lab: Design a scheduled Db2 health check

Objective: Create a schedule and alert specification that catches both failure and stale collection.

  1. Define service objective, metric, unit, evaluation interval, and freshness limit.
  2. Run the check as its intended service account with a fixed Db2 profile.
  3. Record structured result and verify scheduler logs/last-success time.
  4. Simulate missed execution and sustained breach, then test alert routing.

Answer key: The alert distinguishes failed metric, missing data, and scheduler failure and includes owner, threshold rationale, and runbook link.

Cleanup: Remove the lab timer/cron entry and test alert route.

Review question and answer

Question: Why is a missing metric not equivalent to a healthy zero?

Answer: Missing data may mean the collector or scheduler failed, so freshness needs its own alert state.
