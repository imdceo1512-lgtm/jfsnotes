HADR Delay and State Troubleshooting

Diagnose transport, log shipping, and replay delays without making a takeover decision from one status field.

Level: Advanced · Effort: 2 hours
Prerequisites: G-02 and E-02/E-03.
Version scope: Db2 LUW 12.1 is the primary reference. Confirm syntax and monitor columns against the installed fix pack; 11.5 compatibility is stated only where checked.
Outcomes · How it works · Examples and commands · Diagram · Known issues and fixes · Lab · Review

Learning outcomes

  • Separate shipping delay from standby replay delay.
  • Correlate Db2 HADR state with network and storage evidence.
  • Choose safe escalation based on trend and recoverability.

How it works and when to use it

A standby can be behind because the primary cannot ship fast enough, the network is constrained, or the standby receives logs but replays them too slowly. HADR state and log-gap fields must be interpreted with timestamps and trends. HADR timeout, peer window, synchronization mode, log spool, and standby replay delay each change behavior. Forced actions can discard data or create divergent databases.

Decision rule: Compare primary log generation, send/receive positions, replay positions, connection state, network loss/latency, and standby log storage over repeated intervals. Escalate a takeover choice to the authorized incident owner; never force solely because a dashboard says DISCONNECTED.

Worked examples and commands

Capture HADR state on both endpoints

db2pd -db SAMPLE -hadr

Expected / interpretation: Collect at the same UTC time on primary and standby; preserve role, state, sync mode, and log position.

Sample HADR metrics twice

db2 "SELECT MEMBER, HADR_ROLE, HADR_STATE, HADR_CONNECT_STATUS, STANDBY_RECV_REPLAY_GAP FROM TABLE(MON_GET_HADR(NULL)) AS H"

Expected / interpretation: Compare repeated values and log position; confirm columns and units against the installed level.

Check host network and log filesystem

ss -tnp; iostat -xz 1 5; df -h /db2logs

Expected / interpretation: Correlate HADR socket and the actual standby log device; do not expose privileged process data in broad reports.

HADR lag fault tree: Find whether the gap is before or after standby receipt

Compare time-series positions at each stage to isolate the first falling-behind component.

Known issues, errors, fixes, and verification

HADR remains in REMOTE_CATCHUP for a long time.

Likely cause: Initial/remote log shipping is still applying backlog, network throughput is low, or standby replay cannot keep up.

Fix: Estimate backlog, compare log generation and receive/replay rates, and resolve constrained network or standby log I/O before considering role changes.

Verify: Lag declines under representative load and state progresses according to the recovery plan.

Primary reports disconnected while standby remains available.

Likely cause: Network partition, wrong HADR service/host values, TLS mismatch, endpoint outage, or timeout too aggressive.

Fix: Check both endpoints, DNS/service/port, firewall, HADR parameters, and diagnostics; preserve the existing primary role until the split-brain risk is resolved.

Verify: Pair reconnects with one primary and one standby and log positions converge.

Takeover returns SQL1770N or another eligibility error.

Likely cause: Standby state is not eligible, replay delay is configured, upgrade is in progress, or peer-window conditions are unmet.

Fix: Read the reason code and current state; wait for supported readiness or execute only the separately authorized outage procedure.

Verify: A planned operation succeeds without force in a lab and the pair reaches the expected stable roles.

Hands-on lab: Locate the source of HADR lag

Objective: Determine whether a lag trend is transport-bound or replay-bound.

  1. Capture role/state/log positions from both sides at T0 and T1.
  2. Compare primary log generation with standby receive progress.
  3. Compare standby received position with replay position.
  4. Correlate socket/network and standby log-device evidence to the same intervals.

Answer key: Transport-bound lag has slow receive progress; replay-bound lag shows receipt ahead of replay. The proposed next action follows the constrained stage.

Cleanup: No takeover or HADR parameter change in the exercise.

Review question and answer

Question: Why should a DBA not initiate takeover based on DISCONNECTED state alone?

Answer: The primary may still accept writes, the standby may be stale, and a forced role change can cause data loss or split-brain without an outage decision.
