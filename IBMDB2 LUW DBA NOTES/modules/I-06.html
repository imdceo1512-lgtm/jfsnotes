Capacity Planning and Reference Architecture

Forecast database and host demand from observed growth, workload peaks, and recovery/availability requirements.

Level: Advanced · Effort: 2 hours
Prerequisites: A-03/A-04, C-04, G-01, H-01.
Version scope: Db2 LUW 12.1 is the primary reference. Confirm syntax and monitor columns against the installed fix pack; 11.5 compatibility is stated only where checked.
Outcomes · How it works · Examples and commands · Diagram · Known issues and fixes · Lab · Review

Learning outcomes

  • Forecast storage, memory, CPU, and I/O from trends.
  • Account for backup, log retention, replica, and maintenance capacity.
  • Document architecture dependencies and headroom assumptions.

How it works and when to use it

Capacity planning starts from measured peak and growth rates rather than current allocation alone. Database data, indexes, temporary work, active/archive logs, backup staging, and replica replay consume separate capacity. CPU and memory demands depend on concurrency and workload mix; I/O depends on read/write rates, latency targets, and maintenance windows. Include redundancy, failover capacity, failure-domain constraints, retention, and growth uncertainty.

Decision rule: Forecast to the next procurement or expansion lead time with a high-growth scenario, validate during peak workload and restore/reorg drills, and identify the first resource likely to constrain service.

Worked examples and commands

Read data and log growth inputs

db2 "SELECT TBSP_NAME, TBSP_TOTAL_PAGES, TBSP_USED_PAGES FROM TABLE(MON_GET_TABLESPACE(CAST(NULL AS VARCHAR(128)), -2)) AS T"; db2 get db cfg for SAMPLE

Expected / interpretation: Convert pages with the correct page size; archive and backup growth need separate inventory.

Calculate linear growth forecast

Current used GB + (average daily growth GB * forecast days * safety factor)

Expected / interpretation: Use measured daily deltas and state the safety factor, retention, and projected service date.

Check host filesystem and I/O baseline

df -h /db2data /db2logs /db2backup; iostat -xz 1 10

Expected / interpretation: Compare capacity and latency at peak and during backup/maintenance windows.

Capacity architecture: Budget steady-state, maintenance, and failure-mode load

Capacity includes the peak recovery/failover state and the lead time to deliver additional resources.

Known issues, errors, fixes, and verification

Filesystem fills before the forecast date.

Likely cause: Forecast used current average but missed burst growth, index/LOB/temp/log/backup staging, or delayed capacity delivery.

Fix: Reconcile actual daily allocation by path and workload; add high-water and procurement lead time to the model.

Verify: Forecast errors are within an agreed band over multiple review cycles.

Failover succeeds but the standby cannot sustain production throughput.

Likely cause: Standby CPU, memory, storage, or network capacity was sized only for log replay or reads.

Fix: Model full primary workload after takeover and reserve capacity for catchup, recovery, and background work.

Verify: Run a peak-load failover drill and meet the service objective on the standby.

Backup or reorganization window grows beyond available time.

Likely cause: Throughput and concurrent workload were not included in the capacity/maintenance model.

Fix: Measure data volume and throughput under representative load, then scale storage/network or reschedule and throttle.

Verify: Timed rehearsal completes with foreground latency and recovery objectives inside bounds.

Hands-on lab: Build a 12-month resource forecast

Objective: Estimate when a database, filesystem, or standby first reaches its planned limit.

  1. Collect at least several weeks of used-capacity and peak-resource samples by path/member.
  2. Separate data, index, temp, log archive, backup staging, and replica requirements.
  3. Forecast average and high-growth scenarios through capacity procurement lead time.
  4. Validate peak workload and failover assumptions with a measured rehearsal.

Answer key: The forecast identifies the limiting resource, projected threshold date, uncertainty/safety factor, capacity action lead time, and failover headroom.

Cleanup: Planning exercise only.

Review question and answer

Question: Why must standby capacity be sized for the primary workload?

Answer: After takeover it serves production traffic, not only log replay; a smaller standby can preserve data while failing the service objective.
